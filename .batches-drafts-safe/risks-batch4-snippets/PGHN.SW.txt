[4] Credit risk The following sections present the Group’s exposure to credit risk and how it is managed by the Group.
[5] Credit risk is the risk of financial loss to the Group if a customer or counterparty to financial instruments fails to meet its contractual obligations and typically arises from the Group’s trade and other receivables, loans, and cash and cash equivalents.
[6] The Group reassesses the credit risk for trade and other receivables on a regular basis by calculating the expected credit loss for such receivables.
[7] As of the reporting date, no material receivables were overdue (31 December 2019: none).
[8] The Group periodically also reviews its customer exposure and concentration.
[9] As of 31 December 2020, there is no substantial concentration of credit risk (31 December 2019: none).
[10] Other counterparties of the Group are typically regulated financial institutions or institutional investors with a high credit quality and, to a lesser extent, portfolio companies.
[11] Based on its assessment as of 31 December 2020, the Group has not identified any material expected credit losses (31 December 2019: none).
[12] The COVID-19 pandemic did not change the Group’s assessment with regard to the credit risk related to trade and other receivables. (b) Loans The Group’s loans (see note 5.3.4.) are typically granted to various investment programs managed by the Group on behalf of its clients.
[13] The Group reassesses the credit risk of its loans (see note 5.3.4. and note 5.3.5.) on a regular basis by calculating the expected credit loss for its loans.
[14] As of the reporting date, no material loans were overdue (31 December 2019: none).
[15] In order to manage the default risk, the granting of loans is contingent on the adherence to certain loan-to-value ratios.
[16] The Group hereby ensures that the loan to an investment program is classified according to its risk weight and measured against a risk budget.
[17] In addition, the Group has established a system-based loan approval process to control the credit risk resulting from loans to investment programs.
[18] This process is supported by a risk policy framework and pre-defined approval authorities.
[19] The Group evaluates each counterparty with a proprietary risk scoring that includes 20 observable parameters such as credit risk ratings, capital ratio, stock price and return on assets and determines the expected credit loss of its bank balances.
[20] The Group reassesses the credit risk for cash and cash equivalents on a regular basis.
[21] The COVID-19 pandemic did not change the Group’s assessment with regard to balances with banks. 5.4.2.
[22] Market risk Market risk is the risk that changes in market prices, such as foreign currency exchange rates, interest rates and equity prices, will affect the Group’s income or the value of its holdings of financial instruments.
[23] The objective of market risk management is to manage and control market risk exposures within acceptable parameters, while optimizing returns.
[24] The Group may buy and sell derivatives in order to manage certain market risks.
[25] All such transactions are carried out within the guidelines defined in the Rules of the Organization and of Operations (“ROO”) as issued by the BoD. (a) Currency risk The Group is exposed to transactional currency risk mainly on receivables, payables, cash and cash equivalents as well as loans that are denominated in a currency other than the functional currency of the respective subsidiaries.
[26] The currency risk mainly results from exposures in Euros (EUR), US dollars (USD), British pounds (GBP) and Singapore dollars (SGD).
[27] In general, the Group economically hedges foreign exchange exposures related to third-party assets and liabilities.
[28] As a consequence, the Group’s net balance sheet currency risk is limited mainly to its intercompany receivables and payables. (b) Interest rate risk The Group’s income and operating cash flows are substantially independent from changes in market interest rates.
[29] The Group is mainly exposed to cash flow interest rate risk with respect to its cash and cash equivalents held at banks.
[30] Such cash flows are dependent on changes in short-term market interest rates.
[31] Due to this short-term nature and limited sensitivity, the Group currently does not actively manage its cash flow interest rate risk.
[32] This analysis assumes that all other variables, particularly foreign currency rates, remain constant.
[33] Therefore, changes in interest rates would not affect profit or loss. (c) Market risk of investments in investment programs The Group is exposed to market risks (other than interest rate and foreign currency risk) because of its investments in investment programs which are classified at fair value through profit or loss.
[34] In assessing the market risk associated with the Group’s investments, a volatility ratio was applied to each of its investments classified as marketable securities, financial investments or assets and liabilities held for sale.
[35] The Group used long-term data to determine the volatilities for each asset class.
[36] Liquidity risk Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they become due.
[37] The Group’s approach to managing liquidity risk is to ensure that it has sufficient liquidity to meet its liabilities when due, under both normal and stressed conditions, without incurring unacceptable losses or risking damage to the Group’s reputation.
[38] The Group’s long-term contracts with clients mitigate its exposure to liquidity risk.
[39] In order to assess the development of its liquidity, the Group uses a cash flow forecasting tool which is integrated into the budgeting and reporting process and assists in monitoring cash flow requirements and optimizing its cash return on investments.
[40] Surplus cash held by the Group’s subsidiaries, over and above the balance required for working capital management, is transferred to the Company to the extent permitted by regulatory and legal provisions.
